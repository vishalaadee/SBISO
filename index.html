Topic-wise notes

Short revision notes for each topic. Use the practice filter to test yourself on the same topic.

1. Programming, Data Science & ML fundamentals (25 marks, 15 Q)

  • Bias-variance: high bias = underfitting; high variance = overfitting. Watch train vs validation error.
  • Regularisation: L1 (sparsity) and L2 (shrinks weights) penalise complexity.
  • Metrics: accuracy misleads on imbalanced data. Use precision, recall, F1, PR-AUC. Know the confusion matrix (TP, FP, FN, TN).
  • Validation: k-fold CV, stratified splits for imbalanced classes, time-based splits for time series.
  • Leakage: fit scalers and encoders only on the training split. Watch features that reveal the target.
  • Models: linear/logistic regression, decision trees, random forest (bagging, reduces variance), gradient boosting (boosting, reduces bias).
  • Python and pandas: list vs tuple vs set vs dict; dropna, groupby, merge; vectorised operations over loops.
  • SQL: joins (inner, left, right, full), GROUP BY vs WHERE, window functions (ROW_NUMBER, RANK).

2. Generative AI, RAG & Agentic AI fundamentals (33 marks, 20 Q)

  • LLM basics: tokens, context window, temperature (randomness), top-p, system vs user prompts.
  • Embeddings: vectors where similar meaning means nearby points; cosine similarity is the usual measure.
  • RAG pipeline: ingest, chunk (size and overlap), embed, index in a vector store, retrieve top-k, rerank, generate with citations.
  • Hallucination control: ground answers in retrieved text, ask the model to say "I don't know", add citations, and evaluate groundedness.
  • RAG vs fine-tuning: RAG for changing or private knowledge with citations; fine-tuning for style, format or task behaviour.
  • Agentic AI: planning, tool or function calling, memory, human-in-the-loop approvals. MCP standardises tool connections.
  • Safety: prompt injection (direct and indirect), data leakage, unsafe tool use. Use least-privilege tools and input/output filters.
  • Evaluation: golden datasets, groundedness, relevance, toxicity; LLM-as-judge with human spot checks.

3. Application development, APIs & integration basics (25 marks, 15 Q)

  • REST: GET (read), POST (create, not idempotent), PUT (replace, idempotent), PATCH (partial), DELETE (idempotent).
  • Status codes: 200, 201, 400, 401 (not authenticated), 403 (not allowed), 404, 429 (rate limited), 500.
  • Auth: API keys, OAuth 2.0 tokens, OpenID Connect for identity, JWT basics.
  • Architecture: microservices, API gateway (routing, auth, rate limits), versioning, pagination.
  • Integration: synchronous (request/response) vs asynchronous (queues, pub-sub, event-driven). SOAP (XML) vs REST (usually JSON).
  • Reliability: retries with backoff, idempotency keys for payments, dead-letter queues.

4. Security, testing, cloud & deployment basics (17 marks, 10 Q)

  • Security: least privilege, secrets managers (not code), input validation, hashing passwords with bcrypt or Argon2.
  • Testing: unit vs integration vs end-to-end; regression tests; test data management; model evaluation as a test.
  • CI/CD: build, test, package, deploy stages; rollback plan; environment promotion.
  • Deployment: containers (Docker), orchestration (Kubernetes), blue-green and canary releases, monitoring and alerts.
  • Cloud: compute, object storage (S3-type), managed databases, IAM roles, VPC basics.

5. General aptitude and English (qualifying, 45 min)

  • Reasoning: series, coding-decoding, seating arrangement, blood relations, puzzles. Practise under time.
  • Quant: percentages, ratio, average, simple and compound interest, profit and loss, time and work.
  • English: reading comprehension, articles, prepositions, synonyms and antonyms, error spotting.

Interview prep

Interviews for SBI's Deputy Manager (Data Scientist) post have been based on the candidate's bio-data. A 2020 candidate report said that questions came from the bio-data fields: qualification, experience, achievements and hobbies, and that questions were also asked about projects and the resume. Source

Bio-data questions to prepare

  • Tell me about yourself (2 min: education, current role, one big project, what you want next).
  • Walk me through your education and grades (B.E., Computer Science & Business Systems, Srijayachamarajendra College, Mysore).
  • Explain your experience. What exactly did you build, and what was your role?
  • What are your achievements? (1st runner-up, Fidelity global hackathon; CodeChef 3-star; Coding Ninjas ranks.)
  • What are your hobbies and interests? Keep them real and short.
  • Are you OK with posting anywhere in India, including Mumbai? (The advertisement says postings can be anywhere in India.)
  • Why do you want to join SBI, and why this role?
  • Why are you leaving your current job, or why are you looking to move? Keep it positive and factual.

Project deep-dive (from your resume)

  • Snowflake cost optimisation: what was the baseline, which levers did you pull, how did you measure the $1.2M saving?
  • AWS SageMaker MLOps trade-detail extraction: model choice, evaluation, deployment, monitoring, and how you achieved 1,200 hours saved per month.
  • LLM compliance project: the problem, data sources, retrieval approach, how you control hallucination, evaluation metrics, and what you would change.
  • Iceberg lakehouse security: why open table formats, the access model, and how you handled governance.

Technical questions to practise out loud

  • Explain the bias-variance trade-off with an example from one of your projects.
  • How would you design a RAG pipeline for bank policy documents? Cover ingestion, chunking, embeddings, retrieval, reranking, and evaluation.
  • How would you reduce hallucinations in a banking chatbot? Cover grounding, citations, refusal behaviour and human review.
  • How would you secure an LLM-powered API? Cover authentication, rate limiting, input filtering against prompt injection, and logging.
  • When would you choose RAG over fine-tuning?
  • How would you test an LLM application? Cover golden datasets, groundedness scores, regression tests, and monitoring.
  • Explain idempotency and why it matters for payment or transfer APIs.
